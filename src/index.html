Interior · The facility

A weekly escape room puzzle, analysed by people in lab coats. On her lab coat, Dr. Tiffany. Escape room architect, Head of department of highest difficulty levels. She is taking a new hire around.

They walk past ten people who look so fierce and strong, fighting each other until the strongest wins. But he still cannot escape. Still locked.

3 · 27

Next, ten people who look so smart and nerdy, locked in a lab. They band together and group up to break free and escape. Fails.

The clever
7 · 23

Next, ten people who formed three different groups. High tech weaponry. Fails.

The equipped
17 · 13

Ten people trying to break the system. Fails.

The rebels
12 · 18

She smiles as they approach the next room.

Even with the strongest, or the smartest, or the cleverest, the richest, or the most talented, they still cannot escape. Only around half succeed.

So what is the next one?

The last room

Ten clearly deranged individuals, akin to Arkham asylum. Ten different personality and psychiatric disorders.

The assistant is confused, and points out they are not even attempting to escape.

Well. Two are. Seven don't.

Assistant"Are these the ones who already gave up?"
Hecate"These are the ones who almost always always win. Thirty simulations of the most difficult rooms. One of them always gets out, and the rest casually follows."
Flashback · one night

It always starts the same. They are doing nine different extreme things independently. Five different challenges. Always one finds a way, because of the extreme neurodiversity he or she has, while the other eight are clueless.

Always a different person. And yet, no matter how diverse and difficult, one of them always ends up being the best at that task.

Back in the corridor

Hecate answers while smiling.

Hecate"Kid, the hardest problems in life are often not just one dimensional. Space has three axis. Four if you add time. Many more if you add the rate of change in each as its own axis, and the immeasurable, and the unknown, and the opposite side of what is seen and modeled."
Hecate"That's why we do these, though. These started as the room for the misfits that management don't know where to put. The ten people who are the most extreme in some kinda spectrum."
Hecate"You put ten of the extreme outliers of different spectrums, they outperform the other test groups in breakthroughs, no matter the problem. Eight will be wildly wrong and one will somehow find the weirdest solution that no one ever even thought about before. Most advancements in human history are made by extreme outliers, no matter in what direction. This is the power of neurodiversity."
Assistant"Wow. Never thought about it like that."

Hecate smiled, and just said:

Hecate"Of course, kid. Of course. Because you're not an outlier. Your brain is normal, right? By definition you likely will never think about it like an outlier would."
The count

All this time there have only been nine people in the room. It was not said before. Then the assistant asks.

Wait. I counted only nine. Oh my god, did one escape already? They already won. They already won, she shouts in excitement towards the other lab dudes.

She looks back. A lab coat on the floor. The front door open.

Someone yells, "Hecate stole my coat, stop her." She looks back again.

It is Dr. Tiffany.

Afterword

Why the last room wins

The first four rooms are the four things a person is normally told to be. Strong. Clever. Well equipped. Rebellious. Each one is a strategy, each one is coherent, and each one converges on roughly the same place, which is about half.

The fifth room does not contain better people. It contains people who are not running any of those four strategies, because they cannot. Seven of them do not even try. That is the honest half of it and the story does not hide it - most of the distribution produces nothing, and says so on the page.

But the two who do try are not doing a better version of the same thing. They are doing something nobody in the first four rooms would ever have generated, and it is not because they are superior. It is because they were never able to run the standard play in the first place.

The counter on the last room is the only one the story never shows you.

Everything the redpaper later established with evidence is already here. The distribution is wider, not higher. Both tails are real. Seven do not attempt. Two attempt anyway. And the escape, when it happens, does not look like an escape until afterwards, when somebody counts and finds nine where there were ten.

She does not leave them there, either. The last thing she does is turn the taxi around and go back for the other nine.
